Feather River Grappling Series

Rules & Regulations

Clear Competition. Consistent Officiating.

Feather River Grappling Series uses a clear, position-based scoring system for Gi and No-Gi competition. Competitors, coaches, and parents should review the rules before tournament day.

01

Match Outcomes

An FRG match may be decided by:

  • Submission
  • Points
  • Disqualification
  • Referee stoppage
  • Referee decision, when necessary

Submission

A competitor wins immediately when the opponent taps physically or verbally to a legal submission.

A referee may also stop a match when a competitor is unable to intelligently or safely defend a submission.

For youth competitors, referees are instructed to prioritize athlete safety and may stop a submission before a tap when injury appears imminent.

Referee / Medical Stoppage

The referee or event medical staff may stop a match when a competitor cannot safely continue.

Examples include injury, loss of consciousness, uncontrolled bleeding, illness, or another condition that makes continued competition unsafe.

Disqualification

A competitor may lose by disqualification for serious fouls, illegal techniques, repeated penalties, unsportsmanlike conduct, or intentionally disregarding referee instructions.

Detailed penalty and illegal-technique rules will be covered separately.

Points

If time expires without a submission or disqualification, the competitor with the greater number of points wins.

Tied Score

No Overtime

FRG matches are decided during regulation.

If the score is tied when regulation time expires:

  1. The competitor who scored the most recent points will be declared the winner.
  2. If neither competitor scored, or the score cannot be resolved using the most-recent-score rule, the referee will determine the winner based on overall match activity.

The referee should consider:

  • Legitimate submission attempts
  • Near-scoring positions
  • Positional advancement
  • Effective offensive action
  • Initiative and engagement

3-Second Control

Unless otherwise specified, a scoring position must be established and controlled for at least 3 seconds before points are awarded.

2

Takedown

Award 2 points when a competitor initiates from standing, brings the opponent to the ground, establishes the top position, and maintains control for at least 3 seconds.

If the opponent is brought to the ground onto their knees, turtle, or belly, the attacking competitor must establish a controlling top position for 3 seconds.

A takedown is not awarded simply because an opponent voluntarily pulls guard before the takedown has been established.

2

Sweep

Award 2 points when a competitor playing guard or another recognized bottom guard position reverses the opponent, comes to the top, and establishes top control for at least 3 seconds.

A reversal that does not originate from a guard position is not automatically scored as a sweep.

2

Knee on Belly

Award 2 points when the top competitor establishes a recognized knee-on-belly position and maintains control for at least 3 seconds.

3

Guard Pass

Award 3 points when the top competitor clears the opponent's legs, passes the guard, and establishes a controlling position such as side control, north-south, knee on belly, or mount for at least 3 seconds.

4

Mount

Award 4 points when a competitor establishes a recognized full mount position and maintains control for at least 3 seconds.

4

Back Mount / Back Control

Award 4 points when a competitor establishes control of the opponent's back with a recognized scoring back-control position and maintains control for at least 3 seconds.

For scoring purposes, traditional hooks constitute a recognized back-control scoring position.

Scoring Principles

  • Points are awarded by the referee and recorded by the scorekeeper.
  • Control must generally be established for 3 seconds.
  • A competitor must complete the scoring action and establish the required position before receiving points.
  • Scrambles or momentary positions that do not establish control do not score.
  • Once the required scoring position has been established for the required time, the points remain even if the opponent escapes afterward.
  • Competitors may continue progressing from one scoring position to another and receive additional points when each new scoring position is legitimately established.
03

Penalties & Stalling

FRG expects active engagement and sportsmanlike competition.

Stalling

Stalling includes intentionally avoiding engagement, remaining in a position without attempting to advance or attack, repeatedly disengaging to consume time, or deliberately forcing the action out of bounds to avoid competition.

The referee may verbally direct competitors to increase activity before issuing a penalty when appropriate.

Guard Pull

A competitor must establish contact with the opponent before pulling guard.

In Gi competition, this normally means an established grip. In No-Gi competition, the competitor must establish physical contact or control before sitting or pulling guard.

Illegal Grips

In Gi competition, competitors may not place fingers inside the opponent's sleeves or pant openings.

In No-Gi competition, competitors may not grip the opponent's shorts or rash guard to gain an advantage.

Sportsmanship

Competitors and coaches must follow referee instructions and behave respectfully toward opponents, officials, staff, spectators, and other competitors.

Serious unsportsmanlike conduct may result in immediate disqualification.

Legal & Illegal Techniques

Legal techniques vary by age and experience level. Competitors are responsible for understanding which techniques are permitted in their division before competing.

When In Doubt — Do Not Apply It

If a competitor is unsure whether a technique is legal in their division, they should not apply the technique. Illegal techniques may result in a penalty, match stoppage, or immediate disqualification depending on severity.

Dangerous Suplex / Spike is defined as a suplex, throw, or takedown that intentionally causes or creates a substantial risk of the opponent landing on the head or neck.

Jumping Guard

Jumping guard is prohibited in all Feather River Grappling divisions. Competitors may pull guard from standing only without jumping onto or suspending their body weight from the opponent.

Takedown Safety

Throws and takedowns are legal when performed with control and when the opponent is not intentionally driven onto the head or neck. Spiking, intentionally driving an opponent onto the head or neck, or completing a throw in a manner that creates an unreasonable risk of serious injury is illegal.

Youth Technique Safety

FRG referees will apply an increased standard of safety in youth competition.

  • If a youth competitor begins applying an illegal technique, the referee may immediately stop the action and release the position.
  • An accidental or momentary illegal position may be corrected without disqualification when no meaningful advantage or danger was created.
  • Repeated use of an illegal technique after referee instruction may result in penalties.
  • Intentionally applying a prohibited technique to gain an advantage may result in a penalty or disqualification.
  • If a youth competitor taps or is injured as a direct result of an illegal submission, the offending competitor may be disqualified.
  • Referees may stop a legal youth submission before a tap when injury appears imminent.

Advanced No-Gi Only

Heel hooks and knee-reaping leg-entanglement positions are permitted only in the Advanced No-Gi division.

They remain illegal in all youth, juvenile, beginner, intermediate, and Gi divisions.

Prohibited in All Divisions

The following actions are prohibited in every FRG division:

  • Slams
  • Spiking or intentionally driving an opponent onto the head or neck
  • Scissor takedown / kani basami
  • Neck or spinal cranks
  • Striking
  • Biting
  • Eye gouging
  • Fish hooking
  • Hair pulling
  • Small-joint manipulation of individual fingers or toes
  • Grabbing the throat or trachea with the fingers
  • Deliberately twisting or attacking individual digits

These actions may result in immediate disqualification.

Safe Application

A technique that is otherwise legal may still be stopped or penalized when applied recklessly, maliciously, or in a manner that creates an unreasonable risk of injury.

The referee retains authority to stop any technique for athlete safety.

Slam

A slam occurs when a competitor intentionally lifts or elevates an opponent and forcefully drives or drops them back to the mat in an attempt to escape a submission, guard, or controlling position.

Slams are prohibited in all FRG divisions.

Enforcement

The referee determines whether a technique or action is legal based on the competitor's division, the position, and how the technique is being applied.

  • Minor accidental infractions may be stopped and corrected.
  • Deliberate illegal techniques may result in a penalty.
  • Dangerous or severe illegal techniques may result in immediate disqualification.
  • A competitor who causes an opponent to submit to an illegal technique may be disqualified.
  • Referee safety decisions are final during the match.

A Note on This Chart

The technique chart identifies the primary permitted and prohibited techniques used in FRG competition. It cannot describe every possible grappling position, transition, or variation. When a situation is not specifically addressed, the referee will apply the closest applicable FRG rule with athlete safety as the priority.

06

Weigh-Ins

Competitors are responsible for making the registered weight for their division. Please verify your registration information before tournament day.

Weigh-In Procedure

Competitors will weigh in at the tournament before competing.

Event-specific weigh-in opening times may be published separately for each tournament.

Gi
Weigh in wearing the Gi you will compete in.
No-Gi Only
Weigh in wearing your competition No-Gi uniform.

Gi Weigh-Ins

Competitors registered for a Gi division must weigh in wearing the Gi they will compete in.

If a competitor is entered in both Gi and No-Gi divisions, the official weigh-in will be completed wearing the Gi.

No-Gi Only

Competitors registered only for No-Gi may weigh in wearing their competition No-Gi uniform.

+0.9LB

0.9 lb Allowance

FRG provides a 0.9-pound allowance above the published division weight.

Example: a division listed at 150 lbs has a maximum official scale weight of 150.9 lbs. FRG does not round beyond the 0.9-pound allowance.

Missing Weight

Competitors who exceed their division's allowed weight at the official weigh-in may be disqualified from that division.

FRG does not guarantee that a competitor who misses weight can be moved to another division on tournament day. It is the competitor's responsibility to register for the correct weight division.

Official Scale

The tournament's designated scale is the official scale for competition.

Competitors may be allowed to check their weight before their official weigh-in when a check scale is available, but availability is not guaranteed at every event.

07

Uniform Requirements

Competition uniforms must be clean, properly fitted, and safe for grappling. Tournament officials may require a competitor to correct or replace any uniform or equipment that creates a safety concern.

Gi
No rips, holes, or unsafe loose patches.
No-Gi Top
Fitted rash guard required.
No-Gi Shorts
No pockets or hard hardware.

Gi Uniform

Competitors must wear a Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu Gi or similarly constructed grappling Gi.

The Gi must:

  • Consist of a grappling jacket and grappling pants
  • Be constructed from material that allows normal gripping
  • Fit appropriately for grappling competition
  • Be clean and dry
  • Be free of unsafe tears, holes, or damage
  • Be worn with the competitor's appropriate rank belt when belt rank applies

FRG does not restrict competitors to specific Gi colors.

Rips, Holes & Damage

No Rips or Holes

The Gi may not contain tears, holes, or damaged areas large enough for a finger or toe to enter or become caught.

This applies to the Gi jacket, Gi pants, seams, patches, and embroidered or reinforced areas.

If the referee or tournament official determines that damage creates a risk of fingers or toes becoming trapped, the uniform must be replaced before the competitor may continue.

If a Gi becomes damaged during a match, the referee may stop the match and allow the competitor a reasonable opportunity to replace it.

Patches

FRG does not restrict patches to specific placement zones. Competitors may wear academy, sponsor, team, or decorative patches provided they do not create a safety hazard.

Requirements:

  • Patches must be securely attached to the Gi.
  • Patch edges must be completely sewn down.
  • Loose or partially detached patches are not permitted.
  • There may be no openings, tears, or gaps in or around a patch large enough for a finger or toe to enter.
  • Patches may not contain hard, sharp, rigid, metallic, or abrasive components.
  • A patch may not interfere with normal gripping of the Gi.

Tournament officials may require an unsafe patch to be removed, repaired, or the Gi replaced before competition.

Rash Guards Under the Gi

Short- or long-sleeve rash guards may be worn underneath the Gi.

No-Gi Uniform

Rash Guard Required

All No-Gi competitors must wear a fitted short- or long-sleeve rash guard. Competing shirtless is not permitted.

The rash guard:

  • Must be made for athletic/grappling use or similar fitted compression material
  • Must fit close to the body
  • May be short or long sleeved
  • Must cover the torso
  • Must be clean and free of unsafe tears or holes

FRG does not require belt-rank colors on rash guards.

Grappling Shorts

No-Gi competitors must wear grappling shorts or similar athletic shorts suitable for grappling.

The shorts must:

  • Have no pockets
  • Have no exposed zippers
  • Have no buttons or snaps
  • Have no hard plastic or metal components
  • Have no exposed drawstrings or hardware that could injure another competitor
  • Fit securely during grappling
  • Be free of unsafe tears or holes

No Pockets

FRG requires shorts with no pockets. Open pockets are not permitted, even when empty.

If compression shorts or spats are worn underneath, they must also be free of unsafe hardware.

General Equipment & Safety

Apply these rules to Gi and No-Gi competition:

  • Fingernails and toenails must be trimmed.
  • Jewelry must be removed before competing.
  • Piercings that create an injury risk must be removed or safely secured as approved by tournament officials.
  • Hard casts are not permitted.
  • Hard braces containing exposed metal, plastic, or rigid components are not permitted.
  • Soft or neoprene supports may be permitted when they do not create a safety hazard or unfair advantage.
  • Athletic cups or rigid groin protectors are not permitted.
  • Shoes are not permitted on the competition mat unless specifically approved for a medical reason.
  • The athlete's skin and uniform may not contain slippery or sticky substances intended to interfere with gripping.

The referee or tournament director has final authority regarding whether clothing or protective equipment is safe for competition.

Hygiene

Competitors must arrive in clean competition attire.

  • Gis, rash guards, and shorts must be clean and reasonably dry.
  • Uniforms with strong offensive odors may be rejected.
  • Competitors must maintain reasonable personal hygiene.
  • Long hair should be secured in a manner that does not interfere with the match or create a safety concern.

Uniform Inspection

Tournament officials may inspect a competitor's uniform at check-in, weigh-in, mat-side, or whenever a safety concern is identified.

If a uniform does not meet FRG requirements, the competitor must correct the issue or obtain an acceptable replacement before competing.

Athlete Safety

The referee has the authority to stop a match whenever continued competition presents an unreasonable safety risk. Youth matches will be officiated with an increased emphasis on injury prevention.
